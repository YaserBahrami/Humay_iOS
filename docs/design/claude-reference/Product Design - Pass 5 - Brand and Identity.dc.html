01

Brand platform

Derived from the product, not invented alongside it

Positioning

An astrology app for people who want to see the reasoning.

Three living traditions, each kept in its own language rather than blended into one horoscope. Every claim opens to the placement behind it. The tone is literate and adult; the pleasure on offer is understanding, not reassurance.

The six brand principles from Product Strategy stand unchanged and govern this pass: show the work · the page is the interface · color is stroke and state · light and shadow mean something · numbers are typography · restraint is the brand.

02

Naming

Six candidates, one recommendation, honest risks

The name has to survive a specific test the product created for itself: it must sit equally above Western, Vedic and Chinese readings. That rules out anything drawn from the tropical zodiac, anything Sanskrit-flavoured enough to claim Vedic ground, and anything that reads as a single culture's mysticism. What it leaves is the shared physical sky — which is exactly where all three traditions actually agree.

Ecliptic
RECOMMENDED · ee-KLIP-tik · 3 syl
Astronomy, not zodiac Same root as the mark

The ecliptic is the sun's apparent path — the single great circle that every one of the three systems divides. The Western zodiac cuts it into twelve signs, Vedic astrology into twenty-seven nakshatras, Chinese astrology into twenty-eight lunar mansions. One name that is literally the common ground the product is built on, and it doesn't flatter any tradition at the expense of another.

It also sounds like what the brand is: technical, composed, slightly cool. Its Greek root — ekleiptikos, the line on which eclipses happen — is the same root as the approved Eclipse direction, so the mark stops being decorative and becomes an explanation of the name. Internationally it is already a loanword in the Romance languages and reads correctly to a Latin-alphabet reader anywhere.

Risks

Three syllables and a cold register — needs the warm paper ground and Cormorant setting to stay human. Misreads as "elliptic" in speech; the domain will likely need a modifier.

Before committing

Class 9/42 trademark search, App Store name collision check, and a say-it-aloud test with non-English speakers. Not cleared here.

03

The mark

Two circles of equal radius, offset — nothing else

The mark was already established in onboarding: one lit limb of gilt, the rest of the disc in shadow. This fixes its geometry so it can be redrawn identically at any size, by anyone, without a source file. It is drawn from geometry rather than from letters, which is what makes a rename cheap.

Construction · lit limb at left
Rules
Radius — the shadow disc is 0.76 of the box width. The occulting circle has the same radius, offset horizontally by 0.29r.
Lit limb — the crescent left over is filled gilt at full strength. It is never a stroke, never a gradient, never a glow.
Orientation — the limb lights the left side. It does not rotate, tilt or animate to a different angle.
Clear space — 0.5r on all sides, measured from the shadow disc. Nothing enters it, including the wordmark.
Minimum size — 20 pt / 20 px. Below that, use the one-colour cut.

05

App icon

Three variants, one geometry

The icon is the mark on a flat ground — no bevel, no ring, no gradient, no letters. On a home screen full of saturated glyphs, a single dark disc with one gilt edge is the most legible thing available. The mark sits at 62% of the icon box, optically centred.

Light · ivory ground
Dark · ink ground
Tinted · single channel
Rules
The tinted variant carries no gilt. Luminance alone must hold the shape, so the shadow disc drops to near-black and the limb goes to near-white.
No text in the icon, ever — including at launch or in store screenshots.
The dark variant deepens the disc rather than lightening the ground; the boundary is carried by the limb, not by a stroke.
Notification and widget badges use the one-colour cut at 20 px.

Roast

One brand, a sanctioned accent shift, its own voice rules

Roast is not a sub-brand. No second wordmark, no second icon, no second personality — the same astrology, read by someone less inclined to be kind about it. The only visual licence is that Cinnabar replaces Gilt as the accent while Roast is on, and Roast content blocks sit on ink. Everything else in the identity holds.

09

Notification voice

The hardest surface to keep honest

A notification states what happened in the sky, not what the user will lose by ignoring it. No countdowns, no streaks, no "don't miss", no manufactured urgency, no Roast. One a day is the ceiling; sky events that genuinely matter are the only reason for a second.

Lock screen
{{ brandName }} {{ n.time }}
{{ n.body }}
Rules
Lead with the fact, not the feeling. The sky did something; the user decides what it means to them.
No imperative that instructs behaviour — "Do this today" is out; "Worth a look" is fine.
Never reference the user's unread state, streak, absence or lapsed subscription.
Never a rewarded-ad prompt. Monetization does not appear on a lock screen.
Title is always the brand name alone. No emoji in the title or the body.
Banned constructions

"You won't believe…" · "Your reading is waiting" · "3 days left" · "The universe has a message" · "Someone viewed your chart" · anything with an exclamation mark.

Share plates

The identity's highest-volume surface

A plate is a page, not a poster: one sentence, one attribution rule, no decoration. The strategy target — the recipient can tell what app it is without a logo — is met by the type and the surface, so the mark stays small and sits bottom-right. Ink plates for sky and consequence, paper plates for readings.

Plate rules
1:1 at 1080 px. Same composition at every size; no alternate crops.
Three zones only — kicker, sentence, footer. Never a fourth.
The sentence never exceeds two lines at 26 px. If it does, the reading is edited, not the type shrunk.
No wordmark on a plate — the mark at 20 px is the attribution.
Never share another person's placements. Compatibility plates name the relationship, not their chart.
12

Motion signature

The occultation — one move, three uses

One shape crosses another and settles. It is the mark being drawn, not a logo animation: the occulting disc slides in from the left over 640 ms on an ease-out curve and stops at the offset that forms the lit limb. Nothing spins, pulses, twinkles or loops indefinitely.

640 ms · cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)
Where it is allowed
Launch — once, then the app appears. It never delays a warm start.
Calculation and chart reveal — the onboarding ink screens, where the wait is real and the move is the progress indicator.
Rewarded-ad confirmation — a single occultation as the unlock resolves. Nowhere else in monetization.
Reduced motion — replaced by a 160 ms cross-fade to the settled mark. Never removed entirely; the mark still resolves.

Looping here is a specimen convenience. In product the move plays once and holds.
