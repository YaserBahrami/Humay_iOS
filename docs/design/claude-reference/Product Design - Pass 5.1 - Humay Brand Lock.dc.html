Origin, stated carefully

Humay is drawn from the Persian hümâ / hümây, a legendary bird long present in Iranian and Turkish literary and cultural traditions. We take the sound and the association with height and rarity, and nothing else. We make no claim that Humay and the ancient Turkic Umay are the same figure or share a settled etymology; the brand story does not rest on any etymological argument, and copy should never assert one.

Why it fits the product

Two syllables, five letters, one obvious reading on sight in any Latin-script locale — short enough for a 15 px header and a store name. It carries no zodiac, no Sanskrit tilt and no Western astronomical vocabulary, so it sits equally above Western, Vedic and Chinese readings, which was the naming test the product set for itself. It is a proper name rather than a common noun, so it can be owned in a way Almanac could not. And it is warm where the product's surfaces are cool — the identity's one piece of human register.

Known risks

At least one unrelated software/game product already uses the exact name Humay, so the name is not unique and availability is unknown. English speakers may first say "HUE-may"; the store subtitle and voice-over should carry hoo-MAY. Some readers will hear a Turkish or Persian personal name, which the brand should neither lean on nor correct into mythology.

Not cleared here

Trademark (classes 9 / 42), App Store and Google Play display names, domains and social handles are all unverified. Design is locked; legal availability is a separate, blocking task — see 13.

Brand interpretation — internal

Humay means: the long view. Something that spends its life high enough to see the whole pattern, and rarely enough that seeing it is an event. That is what the product does with a chart — it lifts one day into a longer shape and shows the reasoning on the way down.

What the name does not license
Bird, wing, feather, phoenix or crown imagery anywhere in the identity
Mythological framing in user-facing copy, onboarding or the store listing
Any suggestion of luck, favour, destiny or fortune — the legend is not evidence of anything

Wordmark

HUMAY · Cormorant Garamond 300, letterspaced caps — canonical

The wordmark is a setting, not a drawn logotype, which keeps the identity inside the type system and editable by anyone with the two fonts. Five letters at 0.34em tracking give a wide, calm block with no awkward pair — H·U·M·A·Y has no descenders, no round-to-round collision, and the A holds the centre.

03

The mark

Occultation retained · two circles of equal radius, offset

The mark stays. Its meaning is the product's, not the name's: one body passes in front of another, and the light that survives at the edge is the only thing you can actually read. That is the whole argument of the app — a reading is what remains when you account for what is blocking the view, and the app shows you the blocking.

What it means now
Perspective — the same two bodies look different from elsewhere.
Revealing — what is obscured is the information.
Timing — an occultation is a moment, not a mood.
Plurality — three systems watching one sky, each drawing a different line through it.
Retired reasoning

Pass 5 argued the mark shared a Greek root with the name, so the logo explained the word. Delete that argument. The mark is not a pun and never was the reason to choose a name; it is a diagram of how the product reads a chart.

05

Brand story

Internal — not marketing copy, not onboarding copy

People do not read a chart to be told what happens. They read it to get above the day — to see the shape they are standing inside, and to judge for themselves whether it holds.

Humay is built for that altitude. It keeps three traditions in their own languages, sets the reasoning next to the reading, and lets the user decide what to do with the difference. It is precise about mechanism and modest about meaning, because the interesting part was never the prediction — it was the pattern.

The name comes from a bird that appears in Persian and Turkish literature — something seen rarely, and only by looking up. We borrow the altitude and the rarity. We do not borrow the promises: no luck, no favour, no destiny. The legend gives the name depth; the product earns everything else by showing its work.

Humay is intelligent, editorial, precise, modern and global. It is not a mythology app.

The reference may contribute
Elevation — the long view over the daily one
Perspective — seeing the larger pattern from above
Rarity — restraint; not everything is an event
Movement through the sky — timing, not fate
It must never contribute

Luck or fortune promises · destiny · divine favour · "chosen one" framing · royal or crown symbolism · magical-bird storytelling · any claim that the legend proves or predicts anything. The word "Humay" is never explained to a user as a source of good fortune.

11

Motion signature

The occultation, retained — verified against the product, not the name

One shape crosses another and settles. Read through the product it still holds: the move is the app's own gesture of accounting for what is in the way before showing you what is left. It is the mark being drawn, not a logo animation — the occulting disc slides in from the left over 640 ms on an ease-out curve and stops at the offset that forms the limb. Nothing spins, pulses, twinkles or loops.

640 ms · cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)
Where it is allowed
Launch — once, then the app appears. Never delays a warm start.
Calculation and chart reveal — the onboarding ink screens, where the wait is real and the move is the progress indicator.
Rewarded-ad confirmation — a single occultation as the unlock resolves. Nowhere else in monetization.
Reduced motion — replaced by a 160 ms cross-fade to the settled mark. Never removed; the mark still resolves.

Looping here is a specimen convenience. In product the move plays once and holds.
